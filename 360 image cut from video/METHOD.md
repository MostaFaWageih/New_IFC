## 4. Interpolation

- Frames between two keyframes of the same clip are placed on the straight line between them, in proportion to time.
- An interpolated frame takes the lower confidence of its two keyframes.
- Frames before the first or after the last keyframe of a clip are **held** at that keyframe, at low confidence.
- Result: 140 interpolated, 21 keyframe.

## 5. Heading

The heading (compass bearing of the frame centre) comes from the sun: the sun blob is found in the equirectangular frame, its azimuth is computed for the frame time and site lat/lon, and the difference gives the heading. A blob whose elevation is more than 10 deg off the computed sun elevation is rejected. Frames without a usable sun are interpolated circularly between the nearest sun frames.

- Counts: 142 sun, 19 interpolated.

## 6. Model render camera

Each model 360 is a cube map rendered at the frame's eye point, looking at the target (10 m along the heading), **up = +Z, FOV 90 deg per cube face**, then converted to equirectangular. To rebuild a view in Revit or Navisworks, set a perspective camera at the eye, aim at the target, FOV 90.

Three viewers use the same spots and cameras, one model each:

- `viewer/` - **structure** (`2-1319-HAC-MOD-SSC-...STRUCTRE-MODEL`), *built-so-far* view: elements that start at or below eye level; slabs and beams above your feet hidden. Progress status is tracked here.
- `viewer_architecture/` - **architecture** (`1-1319-HAC-MOD-AAR-...`), *design* view: everything within 80 m that starts up to 4 m above the eye (the level being built next); slabs, beams and coverings above your feet hidden so the sky stays open. The AAR file has no georeference; it shares the structural project origin (same storey levels and plan extents; 453 structural columns have an architectural element centred within 0.5 m), so the structural origin is used.
- `viewer_combined/` - **structure + architecture** in one mesh, design view; architecture drawn in sand, structure in its class colours.

Each viewer links to the other two at the same frame. The two new viewers reuse `viewer/img/real`, so keep the three folders side by side (each `start.bat` serves the parent folder).

## 10. Known limits

- **No GPS.** Every position is an AI estimate from what the frames show, anchored on photo times, sun bearings and modelled landmarks (mostly the south tower-crane foundation at x=53.6, y=27.1). Confidence is low for 160 of 161 frames and medium for 1 of 161 frames.
- **Identical bays.** Many column bays look the same, so a grid ref can be off by one bay (about 8-9 m).
- **Clips 018-023 are the least certain.** They fill the ~8.5 min gap between P07 (12:23:47, FL2) and P08 (12:32:14, deck), during which the walker climbs from FL2 to the deck. Clip 018 starts 58 s after P07.
- **Stitching.** The real 360s were stitched with ffmpeg (fisheye FOV 195 deg). That leaves a seam on close objects and a possible horizon tilt, so the real and model horizons can disagree by a few degrees.
- **Heading.** Frames without a usable sun blob get a heading interpolated between sun frames (marked `interpolated`); a wrong sun blob is rejected when its elevation is more than 10 deg off the computed sun elevation.
- **Model vs site.** Temporary works (tower cranes, formwork, scaffolding) are not in the model, only their foundations. A crane north of the deck matches no modelled foundation, so the crane-foundation anchor behind clips 010 and 019-021 (and the P01/P02 correction) assumes the walker's crane is the south one at x=53.6, y=27.1. The structural model only is used.
- **Photo vs video disagreement.** See Photo vs video for each photo position the video contradicts and by how far.

## 11. Recommendations for next time

Capture practice that would turn these AI estimates into measured positions:

- **Fixed weekly routes.** Walk the same route every week, in the same direction, so frame N of this week sits next to frame N of last week.
- **Start and end each walk at a named grid point** (e.g. stand on 18/D for 3 s, camera pointing north). That gives every clip two exact keyframes for free.
- **Constant camera height.** Use a monopod or helmet mount at a fixed height (1.5 m is what the model renders assume).
- **Export with Insta360 Studio**, with FlowState stabilisation and horizon levelling on, instead of the raw dual-fisheye stitch used here. That removes the seam and the horizon tilt.
- **Pair phone GPS outdoors.** Insta360 can tag frames with the phone's GPS; on an open site that is good to ~3-5 m, which is enough to tell column bays apart.
- **Paint grid labels on columns** (and on the hoarding at the grid ends). One readable label in a frame fixes the grid bay with no guessing.
- **One camera, one setting.** Keep the photos (P01-P10) and the video on the same device clock, so the time line stays exact.

How a second capture date slots in:

1. Extract its frames into a new `frames/` set (one folder per clip, `tNNNNNs.jpg`, same 2 s step) and list the clips in a new `CLIPS` data file.
2. Add `keyframes.csv` rows per clip, at least the start and end of each walk (the grid points above make this trivial).
3. Run `python build.py` for that date into its own output folder.
4. Compare by grid, not by time: for every grid bay, take the nearest frame of each date and put the two side by side; `element_status.csv` gets one row per element per date, so `Areas` shows the change in % cast per bay.

## 12. Editing and rebuilding

Three files are yours to edit; the build reads them and never writes them:

| File | What it changes |
|---|---|
| `keyframes.csv` | Where a clip is at a given time. One row = `clip, t_s, storey` plus either `grid_a, grid_b, offset_x_m, offset_y_m` or `x_m, y_m` (site metres), `offset_z_m`, an optional `heading_override`, `confidence` (low/medium/high) and a `reason` of at least 40 characters. Every clip needs at least one row; `t_s` must be inside the clip. |
| `element_status.csv` | Progress per element: `guid, status, evidence, note, source`. Status is one of `not started, rebar, formwork, cast, not visible`. Set `source` to your name when you overrule an AI row. The `Elements` sheet in the Excel file is generated from this file; do not edit the sheet. |
| `photo_vs_video.csv` | Suggested corrections for the photo positions (P01-P10), with the evidence. Informational only. |

Then run, from this folder:

```
python build.py
```

Only what changed is redone: model 360 renders are cached per position and heading, compare sheets and viewer images are rebuilt only when their inputs are newer. A rebuild with nothing changed takes under a minute; one keyframe edit (about 20 frames re-rendered) takes one to two minutes. To open the viewer or the map check, double-click `viewer/start.bat` or `report/start.bat` (a local web server for this PC only; close the window to stop it).
